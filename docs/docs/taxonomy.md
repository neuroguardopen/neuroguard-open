# NeuroGuard Open — Influence & Manipulation Taxonomy

## Purpose

The NeuroGuard taxonomy is a developing research framework for organizing observable characteristics and potential techniques associated with AI-generated and AI-assisted content.

It is intended to support:

* Case documentation
* Research
* Evidence review
* Case comparison
* Dataset organization
* Community discussion

The taxonomy is **not a definitive classification system**.

A category assigned to a case does not by itself establish intent, manipulation, deception, harm, or malicious behavior.

---

# Taxonomy Categories

## 1. Emotional Influence

### Definition

Content that may be designed, adapted, or presented in ways intended to evoke strong emotional responses that could influence interpretation or behavior.

### Examples

Potential observable indicators may include:

* Strong fear appeals
* Anger or outrage triggers
* Excessive emotional framing
* Anxiety-inducing presentation
* Appeals designed around guilt or shame
* Highly emotional personalization

### Important limitation

The presence of emotional language does not establish manipulative intent.

Emotion is a normal component of human communication, advertising, journalism, storytelling, and public discourse.

---

## 2. Personalized Persuasion

### Definition

AI-generated or AI-assisted communication that may be adapted to characteristics, preferences, interests, or inferred attributes of a particular person or audience.

### Examples

Potential indicators may include:

* Personalized messaging
* Audience-specific language
* Individualized recommendations
* Messages adapted to known interests
* Different messaging for different audience segments

### Important limitation

Personalization is not inherently manipulative.

Further evidence may be required to understand why personalization was used and how it affected the recipient.

---

## 3. Behavioral Targeting

### Definition

The use of behavioral information or inferred behavioral patterns to tailor content toward particular responses or actions.

### Examples

Potential indicators may include:

* Targeting based on browsing behavior
* Targeting based on engagement patterns
* Audience segmentation
* Behavioral profiling
* Timing content based on observed behavior

### Important limitation

Behavioral targeting can occur in ordinary advertising and product personalization.

Its presence does not by itself establish harmful or deceptive activity.

---

## 4. Deceptive Content

### Definition

Content that may present information in ways that could mislead an audience about its origin, authenticity, evidence, context, or meaning.

### Examples

Potential indicators may include:

* Misleading claims
* Fabricated supporting material
* False attribution
* Misrepresented sources
* Altered context
* Synthetic material presented as authentic

### Important limitation

Determining whether something is deceptive may require evidence about context, source, intent, and audience interpretation.

---

## 5. Synthetic Media

### Definition

Media that has been generated, altered, or substantially assisted by artificial intelligence or related computational techniques.

### Examples

This may include:

* AI-generated images
* AI-generated video
* AI-generated audio
* AI-generated text
* Synthetic voices
* Face or identity modifications
* AI-assisted multimedia

### Important limitation

Synthetic media is not inherently harmful or manipulative.

AI-generated content can be used for entertainment, education, accessibility, creativity, research, and many other legitimate purposes.

---

## 6. Hidden Influence

### Definition

Influence activity where relevant information about identity, sponsorship, purpose, funding, or the mechanism of persuasion may be obscured.

### Examples

Potential indicators may include:

* Unclear sponsorship
* Hidden organizational affiliation
* Unclear source identity
* Undisclosed promotional relationships
* Coordinated activity with unclear attribution

### Important limitation

A lack of available information does not prove that influence is intentionally hidden.

Additional evidence may be necessary.

---

## 7. Context Manipulation

### Definition

The selective, altered, incomplete, or misleading presentation of otherwise authentic information or media.

### Examples

Potential indicators may include:

* Cropped media
* Selective quotations
* Misleading timestamps
* Out-of-context images
* Selective presentation of evidence
* Recontextualized authentic material

### Important limitation

Editing or selective presentation does not automatically establish manipulation.

Context must be evaluated against the available evidence.

---

# Cross-Cutting Attributes

Cases may involve more than one category.

NeuroGuard therefore allows multiple potential techniques to be associated with a single case.

Additional attributes may include:

### Content Type

Examples:

* Text
* Image
* Video
* Audio
* Mixed media

### Platform

Examples:

* Social media
* Messaging platforms
* Websites
* Video platforms
* Advertising systems
* Other digital environments

### Evidence Status

Possible states may include:

* Initial documentation
* Partial evidence
* Evidence under review
* Additional evidence requested
* Substantially documented

### Review Status

Possible states may include:

* New
* Under Review
* Community Review
* Research Review
* Updated
* Archived

These statuses describe the state of the investigation and **should not be interpreted as judgments about whether manipulation occurred.**

---

# What the Taxonomy Does Not Establish

A taxonomy category does not automatically establish:

* Malicious intent
* Deception
* Psychological harm
* Illegal activity
* Coordinated behavior
* Political intent
* Authenticity
* Causation
* Audience impact

These questions require additional evidence and appropriate human judgment.

---

# Multiple Categories

A single case may contain several potentially relevant characteristics.

For example, a hypothetical case could involve:

**Synthetic Media**

*

**Personalized Persuasion**

*

**Emotional Influence**

This does not mean that the case has been proven to be manipulative.

It means that these characteristics have been identified as potentially relevant areas for investigation.

---

# Taxonomy Development

The taxonomy is expected to evolve through:

* Research literature
* Documented cases
* Expert review
* Community contributions
* Comparative analysis
* Methodological testing

Changes should be documented so that researchers can understand how the framework develops over time.

---

# Research Use

The taxonomy may eventually support:

* Structured datasets
* Case comparison
* Research queries
* Visualization
* Statistical analysis
* Academic research
* Public-interest investigations
* Open-source research tools

The taxonomy should remain transparent and open to revision as evidence and research develop.

---

## Guiding Principle

> **Categories help organize questions for investigation. They should not replace evidence or human judgment.**
